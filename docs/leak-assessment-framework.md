# The Livingry Leak Assessment Framework

**A field guide for OV to run on every discovery call.**

Practice: Livingry Services, an independent practice, Lecanto, FL
Tagline: *"Systems that stop already-earned value from leaking away."*
Site: [livingry-services.vercel.app](https://livingry-services.vercel.app) · Booking: [Leak Assessment call](https://calendar.app.google/4UfTY4vavUc7iQBT6)

Source of terminology: the Livingry Leakproofing Framework (Find → Trace → Seal → Verify → Keep) and the site FAQ, which are the canonical references for how Livingry describes its own work ([livingry-services.vercel.app/faq](https://livingry-services.vercel.app/faq), [livingry-services.vercel.app](https://livingry-services.vercel.app)).

---

## 0. Why this exists

This call has a public name — the site calls the first structured conversation a **System Review** and describes it as "a structured first conversation and observation of your website, response paths, CRM records, customer journey, discovery presence, and internal knowledge," producing "a written identification of the highest-value leak, why it occurs, the smallest practical fix, and the evidence that would show whether it is closing" ([FAQ](https://livingry-services.vercel.app/faq)). This document calls the live conversation the **Leak Assessment call** and its output the **Written Leak Assessment** — the same thing, described the way OV and the prospect will talk about it in the room. Keep the two labels reconciled: if a prospect read the FAQ before booking, "System Review" and "Leak Assessment" should feel like the same appointment, not two different things.

**Design constraints for this framework:**
- **Consistent** — same five phases, same order, every call, regardless of vertical.
- **Portable** — works for HVAC today, roofing next, legal/medical governed pilots when appropriate.
- **Honest** — must produce "we can't help you" or "not now" as readily as "here's the first Sprint." A framework that only ever finds work to sell is not an assessment, it's a pitch.

The FAQ states the review is typically 60–90 minutes ([FAQ](https://livingry-services.vercel.app/faq)); this framework is built for the tighter 45–60 minute version of that same call. If the call runs long, let Phase 3 (Signals) absorb the extra time — it is the evidence-gathering phase and the one most likely to reveal something Phase 2 missed.

## Vocabulary discipline

Use these words, and only these words, for these concepts. Do not substitute synonyms mid-call — consistency is part of what makes the assessment legible and repeatable.

| Term | Meaning | Do not say instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Leak** | A place where the business is losing customers, opportunities, knowledge, or trust it already earned | "growth opportunity," "gap," "inefficiency" |
| **System Family** | One of the six categories a leak belongs to: Response, Recovery, Customer Continuity, Discovery & Trust, Knowledge, Workflow | "department," "workstream" |
| **Seal** | To close a leak with the smallest useful system | "solve," "fix" (fine casually, but "Seal" is the term of art) |
| **Sprint** | A fixed-scope, time-boxed implementation engagement that closes one specific leak | "project," "engagement" (Sprint is specific — a defined package) |
| **Governed Pilot** | A legal or medical engagement run under explicit privacy, compliance, and scope controls, with no legal/clinical advice or automation | "pilot program," "beta" |
| **Founding Partner** | A roofing client (currently) who gets preferred pricing and shaping influence in exchange for hardening a new system with real data | "early adopter," "beta customer" |

Banned words: "unlock," "growth lever," "game-changer," "supercharge," "leverage" (as a verb), "revolutionize," "seamless," any exclamation marks. The site's voice is flat, declarative, and specific — model every script line on sentences like "We do not start by adding another tool" and "We will not invent facts, storm damage, urgency, or eligibility" ([FAQ](https://livingry-services.vercel.app/faq)).

## Call shape at a glance

| Phase | Framework step | Time | One-line purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. SCOPE | Find (setup) | 5 min | Confirm who, why, and what success looks like today |
| 2. SYMPTOMS | Find | 10 min | Surface leaks the client already notices |
| 3. SIGNALS | Trace | 15 min | Look at the live evidence together |
| 4. SYSTEMS | Seal (candidate) | 10 min | Name the top leaks, map to System Families, propose a Sprint |
| 5. SEAL / NEXT | Verify + Keep (commitments) | 10 min | Exchange concrete commitments, or close honestly as not a fit |

Total: 50 minutes core, with 10 minutes of natural slack across phases for a 60-minute slot.

---

## Phase 1 — SCOPE (5 min)

**Purpose:** Confirm the business, the reason for the call, the top-of-mind pain, who owns what, and what "success for this conversation" looks like — before touching any symptom or system talk. This phase prevents the call from drifting into a generic sales conversation.

**Opening script (verbatim):**

> "Thanks for making time. Before we get into anything, I want to make sure we're solving for the right thing today. This isn't a sales call — my job in the next 45 minutes is to find out honestly whether there's a leak in your business worth sealing, and if there is, what the smallest useful fix looks like. If there isn't, I'll tell you that too. Sound good?"

> "First — tell me about the business. What do you do, how long have you been doing it, and roughly how many people are involved in running it day to day?"

> "What made you book this call? What's the thing that's actually bothering you right now?"

**Follow-up probes:**
- "If we solve nothing else today except one thing, what should that one thing be?"
- "Who else touches this — office manager, dispatcher, partner, front desk? Who owns the phone, the CRM, the website?"
- "What would make you walk away from this call feeling like it was worth your time?"

**What to write down:**
- Business name, vertical, headcount/structure, years operating
- The stated reason for booking (their words, verbatim if possible)
- The named owner(s) of phone/CRM/website/estimates
- Their one-sentence definition of a successful call

**What to listen for:**
- A vague answer to "what made you book this call" (suggests low urgency — note it, don't push past it)
- Ownership confusion ("I'm not really sure who handles that") — this is itself a Workflow System signal, flag it early
- Whether they arrived with a specific pain or just curiosity — both are fine, but shapes how hard to push in Phase 4

**Common failure modes to avoid:**
- Skipping straight to symptoms because OV already has a hypothesis from the intake form — Phase 1 exists to test that hypothesis against the client's own words, not confirm it
- Letting this phase run past 5 minutes — it is scaffolding, not diagnosis
- Asking closed yes/no questions here instead of open ones

**Transition line to Phase 2:**

> "Good — that gives me the shape of things. Now I want to walk through the places businesses like yours typically lose value, and you tell me which ones sound familiar."

---

## Phase 2 — SYMPTOMS (10 min)

**Purpose:** Surface the leaks the client already notices, in their own language, before OV shows them anything. This phase captures self-reported pain and maps it to System Families in real time.

**Opening question (verbatim):**

> "I'm going to name some common leaks. For each one, just tell me — happens a lot, happens sometimes, or not really an issue. No wrong answers."

**Symptom Checklist — mapped to the six System Families**

Tick as OV hears them. Use this exact list; do not improvise categories.

| # | Symptom (ask plainly) | System Family | Tell-tale phrases to listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Calls, forms, chats, or referrals go unanswered or get answered too slowly | **Response** | "We miss calls during busy season," "voicemail," "by the time we call back they've hired someone else" |
| 2 | Estimates or proposals go out and then go quiet | **Recovery** | "We never hear back," "I don't know if they went with someone else or just forgot," "no real follow-up process" |
| 3 | Past customers only hear from you when they call you first | **Customer Continuity** | "We should call our old customers but never do," "no reminder system," "a competitor's flyer probably beat us to it" |
| 4 | Website or Google Business Profile doesn't clearly explain what you do or why you're credible | **Discovery & Trust** | "Our website is old," "I don't know what people see when they search us," "not sure AI tools even find us" |
| 5 | Know-how lives in one person's head, inbox, or a binder | **Knowledge** | "Only Dave knows how to price that job," "it's all in my head," "when she's out, nobody knows the process" |
| 6 | Handoffs get dropped between office, field, and billing — or staff quietly use their own AI tools without a process | **Workflow** | "Things fall through the cracks between the office and the crew," "someone's using ChatGPT for quotes and nobody signed off on it," "we find out about problems too late" |

**Follow-up probes (use 1–2 per symptom that lands, not all):**
- "How often would you say that happens — daily, weekly, a few times a month?"
- "When that happens, what does it cost you — a missed job, a bad review, staff time?"
- "Has anyone tried to fix that already? What happened?"

**What to write down:**
- A tick and a one-line real example for every symptom that gets a "happens a lot" or "happens sometimes"
- Direct quotes — the client's own words are the evidence you'll cite later in the Written Leak Assessment
- Any symptom the client dismisses too quickly (worth a second pass in Phase 3 if the Signals contradict them)

**What to listen for:**
- Symptoms named with specificity ("we lost the Hendricks job because nobody followed up") vs. vague acknowledgment — specific stories are gold for Phase 4
- Emotional charge — frustration, resignation, defensiveness — tells you where the political sensitivity is (e.g., blaming a specific employee)
- Symptoms clustering in one System Family — often the fastest path to a first Sprint

**Common failure modes to avoid:**
- Turning this into a interrogation — keep it conversational, one family at a time
- Solutioning out loud ("oh, we could fix that with...") — Phase 2 is for listening, not prescribing
- Accepting "everything's fine" at face value without at least one specific follow-up per family

**Transition line to Phase 3:**

> "That's really useful. Now let's actually look at a few things together — I want to see what's really happening, not just what it feels like from memory."

---

## Phase 3 — SIGNALS (15 min)

**Purpose:** The live look. Move from self-report to evidence. Screen-share or ask the client to pull up real artifacts. This is the longest phase because it is where hypotheses from Phase 2 get confirmed, revised, or discarded.

**Opening question (verbatim):**

> "Can you pull up your website and share your screen? I want to look at it two ways — the way a person sees it, and the way an AI tool or search engine reads it."

**Surfaces to review, in order, with leak vs. healthy anchors:**

| Surface | What a leak looks like | What healthy looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Website — human view** | Unclear services, no proof of credibility, hard to find phone/booking, no answer to "why you and not a competitor" | Clear services, visible proof (reviews, licenses, photos), obvious next step within one click |
| **Website — agent/AI view** (ask: "does this site have an /llms.txt or structured data?") | No structured facts, generic copy an AI can't cite accurately, no schema markup, contradictory info across pages | Machine-readable facts, consistent service descriptions, structured data present, easy for an answer engine to describe accurately |
| **Google Business Profile** | Outdated hours, few or no recent reviews, no responses to reviews, wrong categories or service area | Current information, steady review flow, owner responses present, accurate categories |
| **CRM lead list** | Leads sitting untouched, no clear stage/status, duplicate or dead records, no owner field | Clear pipeline stages, an owner per lead, timestamps showing response speed |
| **Last 30 days of leads/estimates/invoices** | Gaps between estimate sent and any follow-up; estimates with no recorded outcome (won/lost/unknown) | Every estimate has a recorded outcome; follow-up touches are logged with dates |
| **Calendar / dispatch board** | Double-booking, unassigned jobs, no visibility for office staff into field status | Clear ownership per slot, visibility across office and field |
| **One recent lost job, walked end-to-end** | Client can't explain why it was lost, or explanation reveals a dropped handoff, slow response, or no follow-up | Client can point to a specific, defensible reason unrelated to internal process failure (e.g., price, timing, out of service area) |

**Follow-up probes:**
- "Walk me through what happened to this lead from the moment it came in."
- "If I searched for your business on [ChatGPT / Google], what would I find, and is it accurate?"
- "How would you know today if a lead went cold — is there a report, or would you just... not notice?"
- "Pick one job you lost in the last month or two that still bugs you. Let's walk through it end to end."

**What to write down:**
- Score each System Family live, 1–5 (see Scorecard below) — do not wait until after the call
- Timestamp evidence: "estimate sent 6/2, no follow-up logged, client confirms job went to competitor"
- Specific screenshots or notes on Discovery & Trust findings (agent view is often the biggest surprise for clients)

**1–5 Scorecard anchors — use identically across every call and every vertical**

| Score | Anchor description |
|---|---|
| **1 — Actively leaking** | The system is absent or actively losing value on every example reviewed; client has no visibility into it |
| **2 — Mostly leaking** | The system exists in name but fails more often than it works; inconsistent ownership |
| **3 — Inconsistent** | Works sometimes, depends on who's involved or how busy the business is that week |
| **4 — Mostly sealed** | Works reliably with occasional gaps; minor, identifiable fixes would close remaining leak |
| **5 — Sealed** | Reliable, owned, measured; no material leak found in the evidence reviewed |

Score all six: Response, Recovery, Customer Continuity, Discovery & Trust, Knowledge, Workflow.

**What to listen for:**
- Defensiveness when a gap is exposed live (common on Discovery & Trust — the website is often a founder's personal project)
- Surprise reactions — genuine surprise at what's found is a strong buying signal; rehearsed or dismissive reactions suggest low readiness
- Whether the client can produce the evidence at all — inability to pull up basic CRM or lead data is itself a Knowledge or Workflow leak

**Common failure modes to avoid:**
- Spending all 15 minutes on the website because it's visually easy to critique — budget time across surfaces, don't let one dominate
- Scoring generously to be polite — the scorecard is only useful if it's honest; a 3 that should be a 2 undermines Phase 4
- Diagnosing out loud before finishing the walkthrough — hold synthesis for Phase 4

**Transition line to Phase 4:**

> "Okay — I've got a clear picture now. Let me tell you what I'm seeing and where I think the highest-value leak actually is."

## Phase 4 — SYSTEMS (10 min)

**Purpose:** Synthesize. Name the top 1–2 leaks by dollar-value or strategic weight, tie each to a System Family, and translate into a candidate first Sprint — or determine that none of this is the right next step yet.

**Opening line (verbatim):**

> "Here's what I heard and what I saw. I want to name the one or two leaks that matter most, tell you why I think that, and then talk about what a first Sprint to seal it could look like — or tell you honestly if I don't think that's the right move yet."

**Decision matrix — leak value × implementation difficulty × client readiness**

Score each candidate leak 1–3 on each axis (higher = more favorable except difficulty, where lower = easier).

| Leak candidate | Value (1–3) | Difficulty to seal (1–3, 1=hard) | Client readiness (1–3) | Total | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| e.g., missed calls during peak season | 3 | 2 | 3 | 8 | Owner already tracks call volume, wants fix, moderate build |
| e.g., cold estimates | 3 | 3 | 2 | 8 | High value, easy to seal, but owner unsure who'd own follow-up |
| e.g., outdated GBP / no reviews | 1 | 3 | 1 | 5 | Real but low urgency, low readiness |

Priority goes to the highest total score where readiness ≥ 2. A high-value, easy-to-seal leak with low readiness (client not bought in, no clear owner) is not yet a Sprint candidate — it needs a readiness conversation first, not an implementation.

**Follow-up probes:**
- "If we sealed only this one leak in the next 60–90 days, would that be worth it to you?"
- "Who on your team would actually own the system once it's built?"
- "Is there budget and appetite to move on this now, or is this more of a 'know it's there' conversation for later?"

**What to write down:**
- The top 1–2 leaks, each tied explicitly to a System Family
- The decision matrix scores and reasoning
- A one-sentence candidate Sprint scope for each ("Response Sprint: after-hours call routing + text-back for missed calls")

**Exit ramps — when to say "not now" or "not us" (say this out loud, don't just think it)**

Say **"not now"** when:
- Readiness scores 1 across the board — no clear owner, no budget conversation possible, no urgency
- The client's business is in flux (ownership change, closing a location, staffing crisis) such that any new system would have no one to run it
- The client wants to explore but has no authority to commit to next steps

Script: *"I don't think now is the right time to move on this. Here's what I'd want to see change before it would make sense — [specific condition]. I'd rather you come back when that's true than start something that stalls."*

Say **"not us"** when:
- The work required is outside Livingry's active scope (e.g., a request for legal advice, clinical automation, or a vertical with no governed pilot path yet)
- The client wants something Livingry has explicitly ruled out (see FAQ: inventing urgency, contacting non-opted-in customers, automating professional judgment)
- The leak is real but the fix is a tool purchase, not a system — refer them elsewhere rather than force-fit a Sprint

Script: *"This isn't something we're the right fit to build. [Specific reason]. Here's what I'd look for instead: [pointer, if you have one]."*

**Common failure modes to avoid:**
- Naming three or four leaks instead of one or two — dilution kills clarity and the client can't act on a list
- Proposing a Sprint scope before checking readiness — sequence matters, readiness gates the recommendation
- Avoiding the exit ramp out of politeness or hope for the deal — this is the single most important discipline in the entire framework; the site's own promise is producing "we can't help you" as readily as a Sprint recommendation

**Transition line to Phase 5:**

> "So here's where I land — let me tell you exactly what happens next, on both sides."

## Phase 5 — SEAL / NEXT (10 min)

**Purpose:** The close. Exchange concrete, dated commitments. No vague "I'll follow up" — a specific document, a specific date, a specific next action.

**Opening line when there IS a fit (verbatim):**

> "Here's what I'll do: within one business day I'll send you the Written Leak Assessment — the top leak, the evidence behind it, a candidate first Sprint with a scope and price band, and a timeline. Here's what I need from you: a yes or no by [specific date, typically 5–7 business days out]. If it's a no, that's a completely fine answer — I'd just like to know either way so I can plan around it."

**What OV commits to sending (the Written Leak Assessment) — within one business day:**
1. Executive summary (2–3 sentences)
2. Six-family scorecard with notes
3. Top 1–2 leaks with evidence (direct quotes, specific examples from the call)
4. Recommended first Sprint: scope, price band, timeline
5. Boundaries — what Livingry will not do as part of this Sprint
6. What happens if the client says no

**What the client commits to:**
- A yes/no by a named date, OR
- A specific, named follow-up (e.g., "I need to talk to my business partner — I'll have an answer by Friday")

**Follow-up probes:**
- "Is there anyone else who needs to weigh in before you could say yes?"
- "What would make this a clear no for you? I'd rather know that now."
- "Anything about what I've proposed that doesn't sit right, so I can address it in the written version?"

**Language for the "not a fit" close (verbatim):**

> "Based on what we've covered, I don't think this is the right engagement for us to take on right now — [specific, honest reason]. That's not a brush-off; if I built something here I wouldn't be confident it would actually seal the leak, and I'd rather tell you that than take your money and hope. If [specific condition] changes, I'd genuinely welcome a second conversation."

**What to write down:**
- The exact date the client committed to for a decision
- Any conditions or people involved in that decision
- Whether this was a Sprint-ready close, a "not now," or a "not us" — tag the outcome clearly for follow-up tracking

**Common failure modes to avoid:**
- Leaving the call without a specific date — "I'll follow up soon" is not a commitment
- Over-promising on the written deliverable's turnaround or contents to close the moment — stick to what's actually true (one business day, the six sections above)
- Softening a genuine "not us" into a maybe, because it's uncomfortable to end a call with nothing sold

**Closing line, every call, regardless of outcome:**

> "Thanks for the honest answers today — that's what makes this useful. You'll hear from me [by date] either way."

## What makes a Leak Assessment good — boundaries for OV

A good Leak Assessment is judged by its honesty, not its close rate. Hold these lines on every call and in every written deliverable:

1. **No invented urgency.** Do not manufacture a deadline, a competitor threat, or a "window closing" that isn't real. If there's no real time pressure, say so.
2. **No over-promising.** Price bands are bands, not guarantees. Timelines are ranges grounded in what Livingry has actually delivered, not aspirational best-case numbers. Never promise a specific outcome an AI system might produce (see FAQ: "We do not guarantee ChatGPT or Perplexity will recommend your business").
3. **No pretending to know without evidence.** Every leak named in the Written Leak Assessment must trace to something seen or heard on the call — a quote, a screenshot, a specific record. If OV suspects a leak but didn't get to verify it, label it a hypothesis, not a finding.
4. **Always name what won't be touched.** Every Sprint recommendation includes an explicit boundaries section. This is not legal cover — it's the same discipline the site already states plainly: humans commit anything a customer will read as a promise, and AI will not invent facts, contact non-opted-in customers, or automate professional judgment. The Leak Assessment should carry that same restraint.
5. **"We can't help you" is a valid, complete outcome.** If the call produces a "not now" or "not us," that is not a failed call. Track it the same way as a Sprint-ready outcome. A framework that can't produce that answer isn't honest — it's a sales script wearing a diagnostic costume.

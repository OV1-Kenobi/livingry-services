# Founding Five — Five-Minute Video Outline (Draft)

Internal draft outline for an optional explainer video. **Do not embed any video on the
public page until a real, owner-approved asset exists.** This outline uses a clearly labeled
*hypothetical* workflow pattern only. It contains no invented owner, no invented company, no
fabricated revenue, and no testimonial framing. Nothing here is a claim of a real result.

Target length: ~5 minutes. Tone: plain, engineering-serious, calm. No hype words (see the
prohibited list in `docs/livingry-positioning-guide.md`).

---

## 0:00–0:30 — The frame

State the belief plainly: *a system that is unsealed cannot be scaled.* Adding more calls,
tools, or automation to a workflow that already leaks multiplies the waste. Establish that
the video is about one bounded workflow, not an "AI transformation."

## 0:30–1:30 — Where earned value leaks (hypothetical pattern)

Walk a **clearly labeled hypothetical**: "Consider a typical established HVAC operation."
Describe a common, externally observable failure pattern *without any specific numbers or
named business*, e.g.:

> "Suppose an after-hours call reaches voicemail and no accountable next step is created.
> The caller moves on. Nobody inside the company can see that it happened."

Emphasize the words "suppose" / "for example" / "a pattern we often see." Do **not** attach a
revenue figure, a capture-rate percentage, or a person's name to it.

## 1:30–2:45 — The seven-part leakproofing map

Introduce the map as a *diagnostic tool for locating one leak*, not a product and not a
promise. Name the seven layers in plain language (intake capture, leak detection, TradeOps
context, routing and assignment, escalation and follow-through, field handoff, close and
continuity). Say explicitly: a single pilot does not rebuild all seven — it seals one bounded
workflow, usually inside one or two layers.

## 2:45–3:45 — Human-controlled AI, before and after

Show the "before / after" shape from the page: AI may listen, classify, summarize, or draft;
deterministic rules decide what happens next; a person keeps approval where the action affects
safety, price, customer commitments, credentials, privacy, or material business consequences.
No autonomous customer action outside an approved workflow.

## 3:45–4:30 — What a founding partner actually gets

Paid, bounded pilot. One workflow. A measured before-and-after against an agreed baseline.
30 days of monitoring after supervised launch. The story can be told anonymously. No
guaranteed revenue — an agreed workflow and measurement, not a promised outcome.

## 4:30–5:00 — The ask

Request a Private Workflow Review. Describe the one repeated workflow that costs the most.
Be explicit and truthful about what happens next: Livingry reviews the request and, if there
is a relevant externally observable issue, sends a private observation. **Scheduling access is
provided only after that observation is confirmed as worth discussing** — never say a call or
assessment is already scheduled. No public calendar.
